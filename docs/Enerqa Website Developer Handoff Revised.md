### Enerqa website developer handoff

##### Sitemap page layouts content and API specifications

Prepared for the website development team   \|   14 September 2026

We are revamping Enerqa’s website as a corporate website, knowledge hub, data portal and tools platform. The structure should make our role as a project originator and developer clear while allowing visitors to discover useful news, research and data. This document specifies the page hierarchy, laptop placement, page copy and external-source integration requirements for that build.

Use six primary navigation sections: Homepage, Domains and Industries, Knowledge Hub, Data Portal, Tools and About. Do not publish project showcases, case studies, experience sections, work-history counters or project-client galleries at this stage. Retain the offering descriptions, the cross-domain Project Development and Lifecycle Support page and project-enquiry calls to action.

Every API used by the website must be free for Enerqa’s public corporate use. The Data Portal relies entirely on free APIs. Every reading and dataset destination must be lawfully open access, without payment, subscription or compulsory sign-in. A free API that discovers paywalled material does not make that material eligible. Knowledge Hub has exactly two collections: Enerqa Publication and Global Intelligence, each searchable by keyword and topic.

#### How to use this document

Start with the sitemap and shared navigation. For each page, review the laptop wireframes before its segment-mapped copy and API recommendations. The diagrams show successive browser views, including the top and bottom viewport folds. They are layout instructions rather than a finished brand design.

Copy IDs such as C01 and C01N belong to the same page’s diagram. Provider IDs link to the complete API specifications near the end. The approved narrative domain and industry copy and capability descriptions form the core offering. New interface headings and supporting copy are identified as proposed where they complete an undeveloped page. Publish only working data, downloads and tools with cleared rights and tested access.

### Shared navigation and mega menu

#### Header and footer placement

Use a compact sticky header approximately 70 px high. Place the Enerqa logo on the left, the six primary navigation links across the centre, and language selection, search access and a Contact link on the right. The homepage can use the short visible label Home while the sitemap identifies Homepage. Keep the primary label About consistent.

On a laptop, the Domains and Industries item opens a wide mega menu under the header. Use click and keyboard activation as well as pointer access; do not rely on hover alone. Escape and an outside click close the panel. Focus must remain visible and follow the visual reading order. On mobile, convert the same groups into labelled expandable sections rather than removing industry links.

#### Domains and Industries mega menu

![PDF page 7, diagram 1: Domains and Industries mega menu](assets/enerqa-website-developer-handoff-revised/page-007-image-01.png)

Use the exact lifecycle link label shown above. It is a cross-domain method, not a fifth domain. The menu can visually subgroup industries for scanning, but every one of the 13 industry pages must remain directly reachable.

#### Placement within each segment

Within text segments, place the heading at the top left, the narrative directly below it and the main action below the narrative. In a two-column capability row, use matching top alignment and allow each narrative to wrap naturally. Domain and industry introductory actions sit below the opening narrative, with section links alongside or beneath them. Do not float an enquiry button over the text.

The AI input occupies the full available width with its submit button at the right; suggestion chips sit immediately below. News cards place headline and permitted teaser above their source/date/original-link row. The compact market panel places instrument rows above a short sourced commentary item. ##### H01 Project Development for a Sustainable Future

Website heading: Project Development for a Sustainable Future

From an initial idea to feasibility, finance and implementation, Enerqa develops projects across climate action, energy transition, environment, nature, circularity, ESG and sustainable finance.

##### H02 Ask Explore Discover

Website heading: Ask Explore Discover

Search placeholder: Ask a question or explore a topic.

Search guidance: Ask about any topic. Where relevant, explore Enerqa’s capabilities, publications, data and tools alongside a source-led answer.

Button or link: Ask → /search?q={query}

Suggested chips: How can a climate project attract finance? \| Explore renewable-energy feasibility \| What does ESG readiness involve?

H02 AI placement: one input, clear submit button and loading state. Expand to a dedicated answer page on submission. Do not force a capability recommendation into unrelated questions. Use current first-party retrieval rather than claiming the model is trained exclusively on Enerqa content. Inference and supporting API calls must use a free corporate-use service within its free quota or a self-hosted appropriately licensed model; no paid fallback or automatic upgrade. Quota exhaustion falls back to keyword results and an honest status. See the AI provider policy.

##### H03 Global News

Website heading: Global News

Follow developments in climate action, energy, environment, nature, circularity, sustainable business and finance.

Button or link: View All News → /knowledge-hub/global-intelligence

H03 news cards: headline, source, publication date/time, permitted short description and verified open-access full-reading link. Filters: All \| Climate \| Energy \| Environment and Nature \| Business and Finance. Lead story plus two shorter stories fit inside the first viewport. No auto-rotating or infinite-scrolling feed. Use NewsData.io’s delayed free feed and additional legacy GDELT coverage; deduplicate and quality-filter before display. Reject paywalled or sign-in-only results before they reach any page.

Provider specifications: [newsdata](<#page-210>) \| [gdelt](<#page-211>)

##### H04 Major Markets

Website heading: Major Markets

Read commentary on major market developments, investment conditions and economic trends from openly accessible sources.

H04 launch requirement: a numerical feed is eligible only if it is free for public corporate display of the exact instruments and its linked source is openly accessible. No paid API, trial, paid fallback or licence purchase is permitted. A free display-only widget is not a downloadable data API and must never supply the Data Portal. If no compliant feed is confirmed, use this small panel for open-access market commentary only and omit numerical rows and live claims. Commentary uses eligible items from the same free news pool. Correct index-futures versus cash-index names if numerical rows are later enabled. Preserve the first-fold footprint.

##### N01 Environment  Nature and Circularity

Website heading: Environment, Nature & Circularity

Projects and operations depend on healthy environmental, social and natural systems. Understanding that relationship early makes it possible to manage impacts, meet regulatory and safeguard requirements, reduce liabilities and improve project design.

Environmental and social impact assessment, strategic environmental assessment, permitting, due diligence and environmental management planning provide the evidence needed to identify risks. That evidence can then be translated into practical design requirements, compliance measures, monitoring systems and operational improvements.

Nature forms part of this project context rather than sitting outside it. Biodiversity, ecosystems, habitats, land, freshwater, coastal and marine systems can determine where and how projects should proceed. Biodiversity assessment and ecosystem analysis help identify dependencies, risks and opportunities, while nature-based solutions can strengthen climate resilience, restore degraded environments and create benefits for communities.

Integrating habitat protection, rehabilitation, ecological restoration and closure planning into project decisions helps avoid or minimise environmental harm. It can also reveal opportunities for nature-positive development and projects that combine environmental restoration with social and economic value.

Circularity extends the same thinking to the movement of materials, water, waste and other resources through an operation or economy. Circular economy strategies look beyond disposal towards waste prevention, resource efficiency, reuse, recycling, resource recovery and energy recovery.

Technology assessment, feasibility studies, circular business models and investment planning can turn waste and resource constraints into viable circular-economy projects. The result is a more efficient use of materials, lower pollution, reduced pressure on natural resources and new opportunities to retain economic value.

#### P01 Project Development and Lifecycle Support

Projects are better positioned for investment and successful delivery when the underlying opportunity is clearly defined, thoroughly tested and properly structured. An initial need or idea must be developed into a coherent concept, assessed for technical, commercial, financial, environmental and social feasibility, and translated into a credible business and delivery model.

Project development is the common approach that connects Enerqa’s work across climate action, energy transition, environment, nature, circularity, ESG and sustainable finance. It brings the different dimensions of a project together so that technical studies, commercial decisions, environmental responsibilities, financing requirements and implementation arrangements reinforce one another.

An engagement may begin with needs assessment, research and opportunity identification before progressing into concept development, pre-feasibility and detailed feasibility studies. Business and financial modelling then clarify how the project could operate, create value, attract finance and remain viable over time.

Once viability has been established, the focus moves towards delivery. This may involve structuring finance and partnerships, informing project design, preparing tender and procurement documentation, supporting implementation and establishing systems for performance measurement. Measurement, reporting and verification (MRV) and monitoring and evaluation (M&E) provide the evidence needed to track results, meet reporting obligations and guide improvement.

This lifecycle approach allows clients to engage Enerqa at any stage. We may develop a project from its earliest idea, strengthen an existing concept, address gaps in a feasibility study, prepare an opportunity for investment, support delivery or improve an operating project. Evaluation, valuation, transition and exit planning can also form part of the engagement when a project reaches a new phase.

#### P02 The Project Lifecycle

Every project follows a different route, but effective development depends on a clear progression from need to implementation and long-term value. Enerqa’s adaptable project lifecycle brings technical, commercial, financial, environmental, social and institutional considerations into the process at the stage when they can influence decisions.

Clients may engage Enerqa throughout the complete project cycle or at a specific point where further evidence, structure or delivery support is required.

##### P02 1 Needs and Opportunity Assessment

Website heading: Needs and Opportunity Assessment

Project development begins by understanding the underlying need, the people or organisations affected and the conditions in which a solution must operate. Research, data analysis and stakeholder engagement help identify challenges, policy priorities, market gaps and underused assets or resources.

This work establishes whether a meaningful opportunity exists and how it relates to wider commercial, environmental, social or development objectives. The outcome is a clearly defined need supported by

##### P02 2 Idea and Concept Development

Website heading: Idea and Concept Development

An identified opportunity is shaped into a coherent project concept with a clear purpose, intended users or beneficiaries, proposed activities and expected results. Alternative approaches are considered before defining the preliminary scope, delivery logic, resource requirements and criteria for success.

This stage provides enough structure to communicate the project to decision-makers, prospective partners, investors and funders. It also identifies the technical, commercial, financial, environmental and institutional questions that must be investigated before the concept can progress.

Depending on the intended audience, the resulting material may take the form of an initial project brief, concept note, funding concept, preliminary scope of work or terms of reference for further studies.

##### P02 3 Pre Feasibility and Feasibility Studies

Website heading: Pre-Feasibility and Feasibility Studies

A pre-feasibility study determines whether a project concept has sufficient potential to justify more detailed investigation. It reviews the principal technical options, market conditions, likely costs, environmental and social considerations, implementation constraints and material risks.

Promising concepts can then progress to a comprehensive feasibility study. This examines technical performance, market demand, legal and regulatory requirements, institutional capacity, financial viability, environmental and social impacts, delivery arrangements and implementation risks in greater depth.

By comparing alternatives and testing critical assumptions, the feasibility process establishes whether the project is viable, how it should be configured and what must be resolved before an investment or implementation decision.

##### P02 4 Business and Financial Modelling

Website heading: Business and Financial Modelling

A technically viable solution also needs a sustainable economic and operational foundation. Business-model development examines how the project will create, deliver and retain value, including its customers or beneficiaries, activities, partners, resources, delivery channels and revenue or funding mechanisms.

Financial modelling translates these assumptions into projected capital expenditure, operating costs, revenues, cash flows, financing requirements and investment returns. It can also examine affordability, funding gaps, repayment structures and the distribution of costs and benefits among participating stakeholders.

Scenario and sensitivity analysis show how changes in key assumptions could affect the project’s profitability, affordability and long-term viability. This gives project sponsors and investors a clearer basis for deciding whether to proceed, modify the concept or undertake further development.

##### P02 5 Finance and Partnership Structuring

Website heading: Finance and Partnership Structuring

Many projects depend on a combination of sponsors, public institutions, financiers, technology providers, operators, communities and specialist partners. Partnership structuring clarifies their respective roles, responsibilities, incentives, contributions and allocation of risk.

Potential funding routes may include commercial finance, concessional finance, grants, equity, climate finance, blended-finance arrangements or combinations suited to the project’s characteristics. Each option must be assessed against the project’s revenue model, development impact, risk profile and implementation requirements.

The resulting structure provides a clearer basis for engaging potential partners and funders, preparing investment documentation and moving a sufficiently developed project towards financing and delivery.

##### P02 6 Design  Tendering and Procurement Support

Website heading: Design, Tendering and Procurement Support

Once the preferred project configuration has been established, its requirements must be translated into clear designs, technical specifications and procurement documentation. Technical, environmental, social, operational and performance requirements should be incorporated early so that bidders respond to a consistent and complete project brief.

Tender preparation may include defining the required scope, deliverables, qualification criteria, performance standards and evaluation methodology. Bid evaluation and procurement support then allow clients to compare proposals in terms of technical suitability, whole-life value, implementation capacity and risk rather than initial price alone.

This stage connects the conclusions of the feasibility studies with the contracts, suppliers and delivery arrangements needed for implementation.

##### P02 7 Implementation and Project Management Support

Website heading: Implementation and Project-Management Support

Implementation turns the developed concept into functioning assets, systems or programmes. Effective project management coordinates scope, schedules, budgets, contractors, partners, approvals and stakeholder responsibilities while maintaining oversight of quality, risk and agreed outcomes.

Progress reviews, issue tracking, change control and structured reporting allow emerging challenges to be addressed before they undermine delivery. Environmental, social, technical and financial commitments established during project preparation should remain visible throughout implementation.

Support may continue through construction, system deployment, commissioning, operational readiness, handover and the transition into routine operation.

##### P02 8 MRV Monitoring and Evaluation

Website heading: MRV, Monitoring and Evaluation

Credible results depend on knowing what should be measured, how evidence will be collected and how findings will inform decisions. ##### P02 10 Evaluation  Valuation  Transition and Exit

Valuation can incorporate the condition and performance of physical assets as well as relevant commercial, environmental and operational considerations.

Transition and exit planning addresses responsibilities, documentation, knowledge transfer, outstanding commitments and continuity arrangements. This helps preserve project outcomes as ownership, financing, management or delivery arrangements change.

#### External source pipeline

Fetch through server-side connectors and a shared cache, not once per visitor or separately for every page. Store original IDs and URLs, source timestamps, retrieval time, provider, rights status and provenance. Reuse filtered records across home, domains, industries and Global Intelligence. Keep credentials out of the browser.

Apply source quality controls, language/geography tags, domain and industry relevance, URL/DOI deduplication and publication-date validation before display. Respect the NewsData query cap through separate topic baskets rather than an oversized query. Do not treat a country name or generic politics headline as climate-policy relevance.

Use suggested provider refresh schedules in the specifications, request budgets, backoff for 429/errors and stale-cache notices. Loading reserves the card/chart dimensions. Empty results say No relevant updates are available; service failures show latest cached items with their age or an honest unavailable state. A temporary failed call does not become fabricated content. Rights review applies to thumbnails and summaries as well as full text.

#### Data integrity and download behaviour

Every Data Portal value and export must derive from a permitted free API connector and its versioned cache. No paid dataset, paid map/data API, trial-only connector or manual-upload dependency is permitted. Original-source files may be offered as additional open downloads but do not replace the free-API ingestion architecture. Every published dataset must support a free anonymous download; credentials are held only by the backend.

Serve all external data through a server-side connector and cache. Store provider, dataset/series identifier, licence, original unit, geographic level, observation period, release/version and retrieval time.

Publish separate labels for the observation period, source release date and Enerqa retrieval time; none is automatically a live timestamp.

Charts must have accessible tabular equivalents. CSV downloads must contain filtered data plus attribution, units, methodology links and transformation notes. Do not redistribute data unless the original source licence allows it.

Do not fill missing values with zero. Do not silently mix annual and monthly observations, modelled estimates and national inventories, nominal and constant currency, or different CO2e global warming potential horizons.

If the source is unavailable, show the latest cached release with a stale-data notice and original-source link. If no licensed cached data exist, hide the chart and provide an honest unavailable state.

#### Open access controls across the website

The same provider and item eligibility rules apply to Homepage, every domain and industry page, Global Intelligence, all Data Portal views, tool data connectors and AI citations. An API can be free while the linked article is paywalled; such a record is excluded from public results. Validate complete anonymous reading/download at ingestion and periodically thereafter, without bypassing access controls. HTTP 200 or an open abstract is not sufficient evidence.

Maintain provider free terms, quota budgets and last-review dates. Store fullTextUrl or datasetDownloadUrl, accessStatus, accessCheckedAt, accessEvidence, licence URL, corporate reuse/redistribution flags and attribution. Only verified\_open records publish. Terms becoming chargeable disable the connector pending review; exhaustion uses cached results or an honest unavailable state, never a paid upgrade.

#### Search and AI behaviour

Index canonical first-party domain/capability, industry, lifecycle, publication, dataset metadata and tool pages. Return relevant destinations with excerpt, category and source date. Keep unapproved drafts, confidential briefs, internal CMS records and restricted tool inputs out of the public index. External material used by the AI must comply with its own licence and source terms.

Test general queries unrelated to Enerqa, relevant project questions, ambiguous terminology, Arabic queries, conflicting sources and retrieval failures. Relevant Enerqa results are useful when they directly address the question; they should not be inserted into every answer. Generated responses cite the content actually retrieved and never fabricate company work, credentials or data.

#### SEO and canonical content

Use a unique descriptive title and meta description, one H1 and a coherent H2/H3 hierarchy on each substantive page. Natural domain and industry keywords already appear in the approved narrative; retain them without repetition or keyword stuffing. No copy can guarantee a top Google ranking.

Keep Project Development and Lifecycle Support as one canonical full page. Contextual modules point back to it. Domain capability anchors use stable heading-derived slugs; indexed pages should not be thin duplicates. Consolidate first-party article duplicates item by item, publish clean canonical URLs and include valid launch pages in an XML sitemap. Noindex search results and low-value filter combinations where appropriate.

Use appropriate valid organisation, article and breadcrumb structured data only from verified fields. Do not manufacture review ratings, FAQ claims or experience figures. Bilingual equivalents require accurate Arabic translation, RTL layout, language metadata and reciprocal hreflang only for real corresponding pages.

#### Accessibility responsive behaviour and performance
